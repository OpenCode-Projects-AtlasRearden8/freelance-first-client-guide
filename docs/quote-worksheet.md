# Quote worksheet

Fill this out before quoting any project. It turns warm fuzzies into a number on paper. Keep copies — past worksheets are how you calibrate future ones.

## Part 1: The project, in one sentence

> **Done looks like:** _______________________________

(One sentence. If you need three, the scope isn't settled yet — ask the client one more question.)

## Part 2: The client's side

- **Who is the client (person, not company)?** ______________
- **What contact, and how often?** ______________
- **Who else will weigh in on approvals?** ______________
- **What does the client call this project?** ______________ (use their words on the invoice)

**Raw total hours:** _______
**Buffer (+30–50% for unknown unknowns):** _______
**Buffered total:** _______

## Part 4: The floor check

- **Floor rate (income ÷ billable hours, from Chapter 2):** $______ /hour
- **Floor check price** = floor rate × buffered total: $______

If your instinct about a price is *below* the floor check, the project is under-scoped or under-priced. Either breathe the price up, shrink the scope, or walk away kindly.

## Part 5: Value sanity check

- **What problem does this remove for the client?** ______________
- **What is the alternative for them (do it badly, don't do it, hire slower/senior)?** ______________

If the price feels like a bargain against the alternative, it's probably fair. If it feels like a favor, it's probably charity.

## Part 6: The quote itself

- **Quote range:** $______ – $______
- **Timeline:** start ______, delivery ______ (with feedback assumption: replies within ____ business days)
- **Payment terms:** ______ % to book, ______ % on delivery (classic beginner default: 50/50)
- **Revision rounds included:** ______
- **Rush fee, if applicable:** +______ %

## Part 7: Final sound check

- [ ] "Done" is one sentence the client would recognize as theirs.
- [ ] Every deliverable has a verb and a checked-off ending state.
- [ ] The buffer is honest, not hopeful.
- [ ] The range gives the client a choice of scope, not just a choice of price.
- [ ] Terms are in the quote email, not just in your head.
- [ ] The email ends with one next step.
