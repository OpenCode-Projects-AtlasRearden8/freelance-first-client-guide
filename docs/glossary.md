# Glossary

Plain-English definitions of the words freelancing throws at you. Where a word has a "fear version," the fear version is included so you can laugh at it.

**Billable hours** — The hours you can honestly charge a client for. Everything else is admin: quoting, invoicing, learning, and reading manuals like this one.

**Buffer** — The extra time you add to an estimate on purpose. Not padding; it pays for the parts of the job you don't know exist yet.

**Change request** — A written note that says: "Something new was asked for; here's the cost and timeline effect." The politest instrument in freelancing.

**Deposit** — Money paid before work starts. Not a down payment on friendship; it's the client showing they're serious and you showing you trust them in return only after they've shown seriousness.

**Deliverable** — A thing that can be checked off: a page, a file, a video, a call. If you can't check it off, it's not a deliverable, it's a feeling.

**Due date (invoice)** — The date payment is expected, *written in words on the invoice*. An invoice without a due date is a wish, not a document.

**Floor rate** — Your private minimum hourly value (target income ÷ realistic billable hours). Used to sanity-check project prices; not a public number.

**Invoice number** — A unique, never-reused label like `2026-001`. The accounting version of a fingerprint.

**Milestone** — A chunk of a big project with its own delivery and, often, its own invoice. Milestones keep cash moving and endings clean.

**One-pager** — A single-page description of what you do, for whom, at what price, with one next step. Chapter 1 is entirely about this.

**Scope creep** — The slow widening of a project's edges via small extra requests. Treated with Chapter 5's three sentences, not with bitterness.

**Scope** — The agreed list of what's included in a project and its price. The word that appears more often in disputes than any other; define it in writing.

**Statement of work (SOW)** — Formal-name version of the agreement's middle chapters: what will be done, by when, for how much. Fancy file name; same job as Chapter 3's one-pager.

**Testimonial** — A client's short written statement about working with you, collected on delivery day and used with their blessing. Cheapest marketing you'll ever own.

**Terms (payment)** — The schedule and rules of payment: amounts, dates, and what happens when they're missed. Always stated before work starts.

**LLC** — See Chapter 7. In one line: a legal box between you and the business that can carry risk, sign contracts, and keep accounts separate — when operated properly.

**AP / AR (accounting dialect)** — "Accounts payable" is what a company owes to others (including, hopefully, you); "accounts receivable" is what's owed to it. "AP department" is the inbox your overdue invoice probably lives in.

**EIN** — A free Employer Identification Number from the IRS for a business entity. Applying yourself online costs nothing; anyone charging you is selling you a free thing.

**Registered agent** — The named person or service that receives official mail for an LLC. Part of forming one; the companion llc-first-year-handbook explains it in depth.

**Client** — A person who chose to give you money for a deliverable, and who is often busy, decentralized, and compiling three decisions you will never see. History's most sympathetic creature.
