# Chapter 6: Collecting testimonials

Testimonials are the cheapest marketing you will ever own. New freelancers rarely have enough, first-time clients rarely offer them spontaneously, and there is one simple frame that changes this: **the testimonial is part of the project.**

## The ask, timed

Ask **on delivery day** or within a day or two. Not two weeks later, not after warming up. Right at the moment of glow.

The ask works because you're *placing the last deliverable* of the project — a sentence on your one-pager that will help the next client feel safe. Clients understand that frame immediately.

## Make asking easy

The worse testimonial request is a blank canvas: "Could you write a testimonial?" Most busy people freeze in front of open prose. Instead, ask three tiny questions, and use their answers to draft a short quote *for them* to approve. This isn't puffery — it's saving them time, and, in my experience, the easiest testimonial you'll ever collect.

The three questions:

1. What problem did you come to me with?
2. What did you get that you didn't expect?
3. What would you tell a friend before they hire me?

## A template that respects their time

> Priya — great finishing the site today! One small last thing: if you have 5 minutes, I'd love a short line for my one-pager. Three quick questions: what problem did you bring, what surprised you, and what would you tell a friend? If it's easier, I can draft two lines from your answers for you to approve.

Expect about half of clients to take you up on it. Never chase, never guilt — one second nudge after a week is fine; after that, let it go with warmth.

## Getting usable testimonials

- **Specific beats superlative.** "She cut our invoicing time in half" beats "great to work with," every time.
- **Include the start state.** Both ends of the arc — the pain and the fix — are what the next client needs to hear.
- **Legal-lite honesty.** You may quote a client's words verbatim, with their name (or "CEO, non-profit" if they'd rather not give a full name). Editing their words is not OK; trimming is. Show them the final version before it goes live.
- **Line them up with objections.** Look for testimonials addressing price ("worth every dollar"), timing, or first-timer doubt ("she was patient with me as a first-time client"). A testimonial that answers a worry is worth ten that just cheer.

## The testimonial habit loop

1. Delivery day: invoice + three-question ask.
2. Within a week: draft the line, get approval.
3. Add to one-pager / site within a week, tagged by project type.
4. Six months from now: re-use it in your next one-pager rewrite. Testimonials age well; the ache of the question ages off.
